# Mapping aviation AI evidence to a proposal without claiming certification

**Status:** Draft for review, 2026-10-12. Proposed primary query: `EASA AI NPA 2025-07 disclosure`; search metrics **Not measured**. Reader: aviation assurance lead or procurement reviewer. Intent: understand which source topics a disclosure field can help discuss. Business objective: invite review of a source-traceable, informative crosswalk.

**Supporting entities:** EASA DS.AI proposal; FAA AI Safety Assurance Roadmap; EUROCAE WG-114; ARP4754B; DO-178C; NIST AI RMF. **Product connection:** draft `aero-assurance-crosswalk`. **Publication gate:** recheck source status and links on publication day; verify the repo and generated page.

An evidence crosswalk is useful only if the reader knows what each arrow means. An arrow from a card field to a regulator's topic can mean “this field prompts a relevant question.” It does not mean the linked document answers the question, satisfies an objective, or forms part of an accepted certification basis.

The draft aerospace crosswalk records a card field path, public source, source status, reference identifier where available, applicability, connection, and limitation for each row. For example, the Aviation ML Assurance Card's `system.intended_use` and `human_ai_teaming.role` can prompt a concept-of-operations discussion. `odd.operating_conditions` and `odd.condition_evidence` can make operating limits and missing references visible. A proposed target DAL is captured as a vendor assertion; the card does not derive a DAL from an EASA AI level.

[EASA NPA 2025-07](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/document-library/notices-of-proposed-amendment/npa-2025-07) proposes detailed AI-trustworthiness specifications. Its consultation deadline was extended to March 10, 2026. That status matters on every page and in every export. A proposal can guide the questions a vendor prepares for, while the final requirements and the applicant's basis remain separate decisions. The [FAA Roadmap for AI Safety Assurance](https://www.faa.gov/aircraft/air_cert/step/roadmap_for_AI_safety_assurance) describes the agency's direction and distinguishes static learned AI from learning systems that change in operation. It is a roadmap, not a product-specific approval path.

Standards posture needs similar discipline. [EUROCAE WG-114](https://www.eurocae.net/working-group/wg-114/) lists ED-324 as a draft with a target publication date of December 31, 2026, as checked for this draft. A schedule is not a published standard. [SAE's public ARP4754B description](https://saemobilus.sae.org/standards/arp4754b-guidelines-development-civil-aircraft-systems) concerns civil aircraft and system development, while [RTCA's public DO-178C overview](https://www.rtca.org/do-178/) concerns airborne software. Whether either applies to a specific ML system or off-aircraft maintenance tool requires a real scope assessment. The crosswalk names the identifiers and uses public descriptions; it does not reproduce licensed text or invent objective numbers.

NIST AI RMF 1.0 offers [voluntary risk-management context](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/). It can help a team ask who owns a risk and what is measured. It cannot substitute for aviation regulations or an applicant's accepted process. The same separation applies to the MRO card: its field-to-source mapping can remind a reviewer to inspect the organisation's authorisation and maintenance records, while the card itself only records a finding review.

The crosswalk is generated from machine-readable source rows so a changed source status can be updated and reviewed in one place. The method creates a useful audit trail if maintainers preserve dates, source URLs, and explicit limits. It is still editorial work. Someone must recheck a proposal, a standard's publication state, and each field's real-world applicability before citing the table in a buyer or regulator discussion.

**CTA for publication:** Review a crosswalk row against its primary source and the draft card field, then submit a correction with the source URL and the applicable system context.
